## Workstream A — Price Action and classical structures (refs 01, 02, 09, 13, 15)

Study: swing structure, ranges, double/triple tops and bottoms, head-and-shoulders, triangles, flags, wedges, channels, breakout, retest, failed breakout, and market-structure transitions.

Missing: objective swing/pivot definitions, confirmed-bar timing, pattern boundaries, volume/context filters, measured-move math, invalidation, and failed-pattern examples. Distinguish a pattern being visually present from the point at which it becomes tradable.

## Workstream B — Candlesticks (refs 07, 08, 10, 11, 12)

Study: single-, double- and multi-candle formations; rejection, momentum, indecision, reversal and continuation.

Missing: exact OHLC definitions, ATR-normalized body/wick thresholds, trend/location context, close-confirmation, next-bar trigger, invalidation, frequency and expectancy. Consolidate duplicate names and ambiguous variants. A candle label is never a standalone entry.

## Workstream C — Volume Profile and auction (refs 03, 04, 05, 06)

Study: profile shapes, POC, VAH, VAL, value area, HVN/LVN, session profiles, footprint and order flow.

Missing: verified volume-at-price source; exact profile anchor/session; value-area algorithm; developing vs fixed profile; session boundaries; venue differences; acceptance/rejection and retest playbooks. For footprint: classified trade prints, aggressor-side methodology, exchange/feed coverage, timestamps, aggregation and latency. OHLCV candles cannot reconstruct true volume-at-price or bid/ask footprint. The common 70% value-area convention is not universal.

## Workstream D — Gann, Square of Nine and price/time geometry (refs 20–30)

Study: Gann square grids, Square of Nine or related circular wheels, 360° divisions, price/time squares, geometric circles, diagonals, Gann angles/fans, and time-cycle hypotheses.

Required missing lesson material:
1. Source and exact method name for each wheel/diagram; distinguish W.D. Gann's documented methods from Tesla number graphics and later reinterpretations.
2. Anchor selection: which confirmed high/low, timeframe, timestamp and price; how to handle changing anchors.
3. Price unit/modifier and instrument-specific scaling; tick size, decimal precision and volatility normalization.
4. Square of Nine arithmetic: exact formula, degree increments (e.g. 45°, 90°, 180°), rotation and rounding rules.
5. Geometry: square/circle construction, center, radius, diagonals, subdivisions and relationship to price/time.
6. Gann fan: exact 1x1, 2x1, 1x2 etc. slope definition; price/time scale calibration; why a 1x1 is not universal without scale normalization.
7. Time cycles: bars vs elapsed time, trading sessions vs calendar time, holidays/gaps and cycle anchoring.
8. Examples with full calculations, historical cases, counterexamples and failure conditions.
9. Non-repainting implementation: only confirmed pivots; record the bar/time when an anchor became knowable; never backdate a signal to the pivot before confirmation.
10. Validation: test levels/angles/cycles independently and jointly, with costs, multiple-testing controls and chronological out-of-sample splits.

Do not treat geometric alignment as causal evidence or an automatic signal. Tesla number-mapping diagrams must be researched separately from Gann.

## Workstream E — Indicator/scalping references (refs 16–19)

Identify every indicator, parameter, signal timing and market regime. For the promotional scalping infographic (ref 19), recover the complete strategy and independently verify its claimed performance; marketing win rates are not evidence.

## Required completion checklist for every candidate setup

1. Market and venue, instrument, session and timezone.
2. Higher-timeframe regime and location in the dealing range.
3. Exact formation/geometry rules and confirmed anchors (no future leakage).
4. Setup prerequisites and explicit disqualifiers.
5. Confirmation rule and the bar/event at which it becomes knowable.
6. Entry trigger, order type and realistic fill assumptions.
7. Structural invalidation and stop placement.
8. Target logic, partial exits and minimum reward/risk after costs.
9. Spread, commission, slippage, funding and liquidity assumptions.
10. Screenshot/example set: valid, failed, ambiguous and counterexamples.
11. Backtest with chronological split, out-of-sample evaluation and no lookahead.
12. Forward/paper observation and versioned change log.

## Guardrails

- A diagram is a learning reference, not evidence of profitability.
- POC is not guaranteed to act as a magnet; VAH/VAL depend on profile period and algorithm.
- OHLCV cannot reconstruct true volume-at-price or true bid/ask footprint.
- A Gann angle depends on calibrated price/time scale; visual 45° on an arbitrary chart is not automatically 1x1.
- Do not conflate Tesla number mapping with Gann methods.
- Pattern and geometry detection must use only information available at decision time; confirmed pivots are delayed until right-side confirmation.
- No image-derived rule may place or authorize an order.
- Do not mark a setup validated until realistic costs, chronological out-of-sample testing and forward observation support it.
