# TIA — Institutional Operating Model

**Status:** Owner-approved foundation for implementation  
**Parent institution:** Trade Intelligence Agency (TIA)  
**Product:** AI Trading Dashboard  
**Ownership mark:** 6868 (subtle signature only; never a visible parent-company title)

## 1. Institutional position

TIA is the institution and operating framework. The AI Trading Dashboard is one product and operational interface within TIA; it is not the institution itself. TIA is designed as a multi-desk financial intelligence organization with shared governance, evidence standards, research memory, specialist workflows, and a central decision process.

The visual concepts supplied by the owner depict a future institutional environment (command rooms, analyst workstations, data walls, network structures, radial analytical interfaces and market visualizations). They are creative direction and spatial/product inspiration, not a requirement to reproduce a literal office photograph or copy a reference composition.

## 2. Approved seven-function model

The following seven functions are the approved institutional division of responsibility:

1. **Trading Intelligence** — central synthesis and orchestration. Integrates specialist outputs, links catalysts to price behavior, compares scenarios, ranks opportunities, and communicates the institution's current view. It coordinates the other desks; it does not fabricate missing evidence or override their domain checks.
2. **Market Intelligence** — macroeconomics, central banks, scheduled events, geopolitical developments, company/asset catalysts, market sessions, cross-asset relationships, and news-to-price analysis.
3. **Technical Intelligence** — price action, market structure, SMC/ICT, Elliott Wave, Gann, multi-timeframe context, levels, trend and scenario invalidation.
4. **Order Flow & Liquidity** — footprint, exchange/broker delta, DOM, Time & Sales, volume profile, liquidity, large orders and options positioning where reliable feeds exist. Every output must state whether it is exchange, venue, broker, derived, delayed or unavailable.
5. **Quant & Risk Intelligence** — research design, statistical validation, backtesting, out-of-sample testing, walk-forward analysis, transaction costs, slippage, drawdown, sizing, exposure and portfolio constraints.
6. **Strategy & Research Intelligence** — continuous research intake, strategy discovery, indicator evaluation/development, academic and practitioner research, experiment tracking, reproducibility and evidence-based promotion of ideas.
7. **Trade Decision & Performance Intelligence** — opportunity challenge and ranking, trade-plan construction, pre-trade review, execution-independent decision records, post-trade review, attribution and learning.

These are seven institutional functions, not a requirement for seven separate AI model instances. They may be implemented as services, workflows or role-based agents behind shared orchestration.

## 3. Operating hierarchy

- **Institutional governance:** owner authority, policy, risk limits, data permissions, audit and approval boundaries.
- **Central intelligence:** Trading Intelligence coordinates the seven functions and publishes the consolidated intelligence brief.
- **Specialist desks:** the six domain desks contribute evidence, analysis, challenges and recommendations within their mandates.
- **Shared institutional services:** evidence registry, knowledge/academy, research intake, meeting journal, task queue, provider adapters, experiment registry and audit trail.
- **Products and interfaces:** AI Trading Dashboard, Radar, Workspace, Technical Office, Order Flow Desk, Strategy Lab and future products. Interfaces expose capabilities; they do not become separate competing sources of truth.

## 4. Standard intelligence cycle

1. Intake data, user questions, research, documents, videos and market events.
2. Normalize symbols, instruments, timeframes, units, timestamps and source metadata.
3. Validate evidence quality, freshness, coverage and licensing/permission.
4. Route work to the relevant specialist functions.
5. Produce independent specialist findings and explicit uncertainty.
6. Cross-challenge contradictory claims and identify missing evidence.
7. Generate alternative scenarios with triggers, invalidation and confidence rationale.
8. Run quantitative and risk checks where the question is testable.
9. Rank viable options and explain why the leading option ranks first.
10. Present a decision brief for human review; record approval or rejection.
11. Capture outcome and post-decision review; update knowledge only with traceable evidence.

Automation is expected for permitted intake, monitoring, analysis, testing, comparison, ranking and reporting. No autonomous live order placement, account mutation or irreversible external action is authorized by this charter. Human approval remains mandatory for live trading actions.

## 5. Institutional evidence rules

Every displayed metric or claim should carry, where applicable:
- source/provider and instrument/symbol;
- observed and fetched timestamps, with timezone;
- unit, timeframe, coverage and methodology;
- quality state (verified, partial, estimated, stale, mock or unavailable);
- whether the value is observed, calculated, inferred or forecast;
- limitations and known data gaps.

Never display mock data as live. Never replace missing values with zero. Do not describe one broker's DOM as consolidated/global market depth. Do not invent historical order-book depth from OHLCV candles. Distinguish exchange delta from candle-derived estimates. Correlation is not causation. If evidence is inadequate, the system must say so and withhold a trade conclusion rather than manufacture certainty.

## 6. Existing systems: integrate, do not duplicate

The existing Radar, Workspace, Technical Office, XPIX modules, Strategy Lab, Research Hub, Academy/knowledge base, Order Flow Desk and meeting-room records are candidate source systems. Before implementing a new capability, map its owner, data contract, API, UI entry point, tests and current branch. Extend or connect the authoritative implementation instead of creating a parallel copy.

The Academy/knowledge base and meeting journal should serve as shared institutional memory and workflow infrastructure, not a second standalone institution. Existing indicator work remains independently testable and is surfaced through Technical Intelligence when validated.

## 7. Product and market coverage

The architecture should support Spot, Futures, Options, Gold, Forex, MT5-connected workflows, funded-account constraints and equities/indices/commodities as provider coverage permits. Scalping, day trading, swing and position horizons should be explicit strategy contexts. Do not imply universal coverage when a provider or instrument feed is absent.

## 8. Visual identity and environment direction

The owner-supplied standalone logo is the geometry reference. The seven must remain a clean 7, without the unwanted right-side notch/kink/extra fragment. Preserve the approved I/A geometry and proportions; do not redraw, reinterpret or replace the master mark during interface implementation.

The identity card establishes the black/near-black foundation, restrained gold/amber range, warm ivory logo treatment, typography and fine-line framing. Gold is the primary accent; orange is restrained. The 6868 mark remains a quiet ownership signature, not the institution's visible name. Do not introduce a "77" motif or "CAPITAL" descriptor.

The supplied command-room, spherical trading-room, radial-interface, network-mesh, heatmap, candlestick and data-wall images are conceptual references for future environment and product design. Translate their principles into an original, legible interface: layered information hierarchy, controlled glow, precise grids, restrained motion, readable charts, responsive layouts and accessible contrast. Avoid decorative effects that impair analytical clarity or chart performance.

## 9. Implementation sequence

### Phase A — foundation and integration map
- Establish this charter and the seven-function responsibility matrix.
- Inventory current modules, branches, MRs, data providers, tests and deployment state.
- Map each existing capability to one accountable desk and one authoritative implementation.
- Resolve overlapping Academy, TIA, Radar, Workspace, Research Hub and Order Flow work before merging.

### Phase B — institutional command center
- Make the central Trading Intelligence view a synthesis surface, not a duplicate of every desk.
- Show institution status, market regime, evidence health, active questions, ranked scenarios, risk state, unresolved conflicts and recent decisions.
- Give each specialist function a clear workspace and handoff contract.
- Make the meeting room/journal append-only and traceable.

### Phase C — evidence and orchestration
- Standardize evidence contracts and provider error visibility.
- Route work through explicit tasks and bounded specialist outputs.
- Add conflict detection, scenario ranking and human approval records.
- Keep provider access cached and rate-limited; expose last update and failure state.

### Phase D — validated intelligence products
- Connect the technical and order-flow engines only after parity and feed validation.
- Complete strategy and quant validation workflows with costs and out-of-sample evidence.
- Add performance attribution and post-trade learning.
- Expand instruments and time horizons only as data quality permits.

### Phase E — institutional environment and product family
- Evolve the dashboard into a family of connected workspaces and institutional screens.
- Build the visual environment from the approved identity system and concept references.
- Test desktop, tablet and mobile; maintain chart readability and responsive information density.

## 10. Governance and release gates

- Work on feature branches and use merge requests.
- Do not merge a branch solely because its title or documentation looks complete.
- Require code review, focused tests, regression checks and documented limitations.
- No claim of passing CI unless a pipeline actually ran and passed.
- No claim of production deployment without deployment evidence.
- Keep experimental, mock-tested and provider-dependent features visibly labeled.
- The owner retains approval authority over identity changes, risk policy changes, live trading actions and production releases.
